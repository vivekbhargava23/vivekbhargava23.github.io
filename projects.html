Track B · Production quant research

Each engagement below was delivered end to end, from data extraction through model design to deployment, in Agile teams (GitLab, Jira, CI/CD). Clients are described by sector only.

B1 · lead

Risk-engine re-engineering

Major Luxembourg bank · MATLAB → Python
Live in production
VaR · CVaR · ESMixed-frequencyCI/CD
  • Led re-engineering of a legacy MATLAB risk engine into a production Python library now used by the bank's risk team.
  • Implemented 30+ risk and return metrics — VaR, CVaR, Expected Shortfall, Sharpe / Sortino / Calmar, drawdown-based indicators — at full numerical parity with the legacy system.
  • Built a mixed-frequency time-series synchronisation layer handling daily/monthly/quarterly data, missing values, irregular reporting periods, and calendar alignment.

B2 · lead

Strategic asset allocation & decision support

German life insurer · multi-criteria optimisation
Live in production
4-objective ParetoTchebycheffR Shiny
  • Built a strategic asset allocation framework using multi-criteria optimisation across 26 asset classes for an insurance balance sheet, balancing four competing objectives: expected return, volatility, Solvency II regulatory-capital ratio, and L1-norm transaction cost.
  • Applied Weighted Tchebycheff scalarisation with a hyperbox decomposition to generate well-distributed Pareto-optimal portfolios.
  • Owned the R Shiny decision-support layer — radar plots, constraint sliders, Pareto scenario filtering — for transparent exploration of trade-offs across market and regulatory scenarios.
  • Built the feeding data pipeline, including regulatory-capital and HGB/IFRS accounting outputs, integrated with stakeholder-facing explanations for human-in-the-loop decisions.

B5

Vehicle failure prediction

High-performance automotive OEM · survival analysis
WeibullRandom Survival ForestsValidity boundaries
  • Developed survival models on ~108,000 vehicle records (~13,000 observed failures): a Weibull parametric baseline for hazard estimation, extended with Random Survival Forests to capture nonlinear covariate effects without imposing proportional hazards.
  • Documented model validity boundaries — RSF survival probability flattens beyond ~1,300 days — to prevent overconfident extrapolation.
  • Communicated uncertainty and limits to engineering and risk stakeholders for responsible use in warranty and service decisions.
